## 100H or Intense 72H: the decision most people are really making

Both are the same price on Clinique's US site — **US$31.00 for 1 oz** — which is exactly why the choice trips people up.

The 100H Hydrator is a gel-cream. It's oil-free, spreads thin, sinks in fast, and works under makeup without pilling. Clinique lists it for every skin type, oily included. If your skin feels tight and looks dull rather than actively flaky, this is the one.

The Intense 72H is a cream-gel with activated aloe water, caffeine, three types of lipids, and cica. It's positioned at dry and very dry skin, and the formula is aimed at barrier support rather than pure water replenishment. Clinique's page notes the product was tested on women in Siberia during winter, which is a memorable way of saying "cold-weather dry skin," and cites consumer testing on 106 women over four weeks. Those are brand-run studies, so read them as Clinique's claims rather than independent research.

A reasonable rule: combination and oily skin → 100H. Dry, tight, winter-damaged, or barrier-irritated skin → Intense 72H. Buying both and alternating isn't wrong, but it doubles a bill that didn't need doubling.

## What the "100 hours" number means

The 100-hour figure is a claim about how long the formula keeps skin hydrated in testing, not a promise that you apply it once a week. In practice it's a gel moisturizer you use morning and night like any other. What's genuinely useful in the formula notes: it's fragrance-free, oil-free, alcohol-free, and free of parabens, phthalates, and synthetic colours, and Clinique states it's safe for sensitive skin. That combination is why it shows up in routines alongside retinoids and acids, where a rich, fragranced cream often doesn't fit.

The Intense version adds a lipid trio, which is the part that matters if your problem is moisture *escaping* rather than moisture missing. Humectant gels like the 100H pull water in; lipid-heavy creams keep it there. Knowing which problem you have saves you from buying the wrong one twice.

## The rules that decide whether you actually get paid

Most cashback complaints trace back to steps people didn't know existed. Clinique is one of the fussier partners on this front.

- **Start from ShopBack every single time.** Each order needs its own click-through. Navigating straight to the store — even a store you've shopped through before — breaks tracking for that purchase.
- **Clear your cart and close the Clinique app first.** Clinique's own cashback terms state that you need to empty your basket, shut the app, then open ShopBack and visit Clinique from there, for every order.
- **Accept all cookies.** The terms are blunt about this: Clinique can only confirm a transaction to ShopBack if you accept the store's cookies.
- **Don't pay with gift cards or vouchers.** Amounts covered by gift cards, store credit, or promo codes are excluded from the cashback calculation. If the whole order is paid that way, the cashback is zero.
- **Skip codes that aren't listed on the platform.** Using a promo code featured outside ShopBack voids cashback on Clinique in some markets.
- **Fees, taxes, and shipping don't count.** Neither do rejected, cancelled, refunded, or returned orders. Partial returns get prorated — in both directions.
- **Turn off ad blockers and VPNs.** Clicking through a Facebook or Google ad mid-session can overwrite the affiliate cookie and kill tracking.
- **Restart from ShopBack if payment fails.** A failed payment mid-checkout is a common point where tracking breaks.
- **Expect a wait.** Tracking shows up in a couple of days; confirmation can take around 90 days, because Clinique waits out the return window before approving.
